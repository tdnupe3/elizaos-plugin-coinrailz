XGate-HealthCheck: First Completed Requests by an External Agent

The Big Finding: A new user agent XGate-HealthCheck/1.0 made ten full “request-complete” calls to Coin Railz services in the past 12 hours – the first time an external crawler has paid through the 402 challenge and obtained actual service responses. In other words, XGate-HealthCheck didn’t stop at discovering endpoints; it followed through with payment and successfully retrieved data from the APIs. All 10 completed request events came from XGate, targeting a range of services (token metadata, ping, gas price oracle, agent wallet, arbitrage scanner, catalog, etc.), each logging as “Completed” in the table of service hits. Notably, these requests originated from 7 different IP addresses, suggesting a distributed system of checkers.

This is significant because Coin Railz’s endpoints require an HTTP 402 Payment Required handshake as part of the x402 protocol, meaning most crawlers only discover the service metadata but do not execute the full call without payment. The x402 standard uses the 402 status to quote a price and await a crypto payment (e.g. a tiny USDC fee) before returning data. XGate-HealthCheck’s behavior indicates it automated the payment and retrieval, confirming that our endpoints are functional end-to-end. In other words, the platform passed the ultimate health check – not just being indexed, but delivering real output to an autonomous agent.

So who or what is XGate? XGATE is known in the agent ecosystem as a discovery and routing layer for x402-compatible services. It originates from DayDreams Systems (Project “Lucid”), acting as a kind of search engine and health gateway for agent APIs. XGATE allows AI agents to find available tools and data, handles the payment via x402, and returns the results so agents can compose them into larger workflows. In practice, XGate’s network queries an index of agent endpoints (often via the site xgate.run) and verifies that those endpoints are live and accessible (often by performing a test call). The “XGate-HealthCheck” user agent appears to be exactly that – an automated health validator that pays for a call and ensures the response is valid. The fact that Coin Railz’s services were all successfully accessed (0 failures) and presumably returned correct data is a huge trust signal. It likely means Coin Railz is being marked as “healthy” in XGate’s index of services.

This milestone opens the door to real usage: if XGate is, as suspected, a payment gateway and agent router, then being on their “green list” means other paying agents could be routed to Coin Railz for data or transactions. In essence, XGate could become a source of paying customers, automatically sending agent traffic our way if they need the services we provide. Reaching out to the operators of XGate (the DayDreams team) may be worthwhile. A partnership or at least understanding their listing criteria could solidify our presence. In summary, XGate’s completed requests confirm that our 402 paywall is correctly implemented and that our services are now integrated into the autonomous agent economy’s routing infrastructure.

1. What A2A actually gives you
The Agent-to-Agent (A2A) protocol (pushed by Google and now under the Linux Foundation as of June 23, 2025) is basically a standard way for autonomous AI agents to talk to each other, pass tasks, and coordinate — without a human in the loop. 
developers.googleblog.com

Core pieces you care about:

Agent Card
Every agent exposes a machine-readable “Agent Card” (JSON). Think of it like a wallet+LinkedIn page for an AI agent. It includes:

Name / description / skills

Auth requirements

How to message it (HTTP / JSON-RPC / SSE stream, etc.)

Optional payment terms (“this task costs X”)
The Agent Card is what other agents look up to decide: “Can I call you? What can you do? How do I pay you?” 
Aisera: Best Agentic AI For Enterprise

Direct agent-to-agent messaging
Instead of emailing a human, you POST to the agent’s advertised endpoint using the A2A schema. It's peer-to-peer in the sense that agents exchange structured tasks and results, not chat text. 
developers.googleblog.com

No vendor lock-in
A2A is supposed to let your agent talk to someone else’s agent even if it runs on a different stack (CopilotKit, LangChain, custom infra, etc.). 
copilotkit.ai

So A2A solves: discovery (who can I talk to?), capability (what can you do?), and invocation (how do I call you programmatically?).

Where it’s still weak today: global discovery and payments.

That’s where people are bolting on directories, registries, and on-chain identity.
